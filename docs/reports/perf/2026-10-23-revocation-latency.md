# Role-revocation latency — 2026-10-23

Generated by `tests/system/revocation_latency.py` (LLD-02 §8 targets, §14 method).

## Environment

Local Compose stack, single host. One authz-svc and one orgs-svc process
(uvicorn, no replicas), isolated test DB/vhost (`make test-env`), fake IdP.
Git `6b81e14` (uncommitted changes). Not production-representative.

## Workload

200 trials per scenario across 10 concurrent fixtures
(one org each). Latency = from sending the triggering request until the
member's first 403, polled every 25 ms.

| Scenario | Trials | p50 | p95 | p99 | max | Target p99 | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| direct | 200 | 0.222 | 0.259 | 0.267 | 0.294 | 5 | pass |
| membership | 200 | 0.230 | 0.279 | 0.308 | 0.317 | 5 | pass |

## Analysis

**Question:** do role revocations reach enforcement within LLD-02 §8's
targets, through the real event pipeline? This is a delivery-plan M2 exit
gate ("Role-revocation latency is measured against its target").

**Answer: yes, all three targets met, with wide margins on the
watcher-on paths.**

- **direct / membership (~0.22 s p50, ≤0.32 s max).** Almost all of it is
  the policy watcher's 200 ms debounce plus one reload. The membership path
  crosses five hops (orgs commit → outbox relay → RabbitMQ → authz consumer
  → watcher) and costs only ~10 ms more than direct, so the relay's NOTIFY
  fast path and the consumer add little.
- **watcher-off (~10 s, tightly clustered).** This is the 10 s polling
  interval, and the tight clustering is a property of the trial loop, not
  of polling. Each trial's restore is only observed on a poll tick, and the
  next revoke fires immediately after, so every revoke lands just after a
  tick and waits nearly a full interval. The scenario therefore measures
  the polling fallback's **worst case**, which is the number that matters
  against the 15 s bound.

## Findings

- **Publish-before-commit (deviation from LLD-02 §7 step 8).** authz-svc
  publishes `authz:policy` from inside the route handler, *before* the
  request's transaction commits in dependency teardown. LLD-02 says "after
  commit". A watcher that reloads before the commit lands reads the old
  policy and stays stale until the next poll (~10 s). Across 400 watcher-on
  trials no such tail appeared: the 200 ms debounce gives the commit time
  to land. That shows the race doesn't bite on an idle system, **not** that
  it can't under load (a commit slower than 200 ms). Recommended fix:
  publish from an after-commit hook.

## Limitations

- One process per service, no replicas, single host, no background load.
  Production adds network hops and contention; the M4 baselines (LLD-14 §6)
  are where this gets re-measured under load.
- The clock starts when the triggering request is *sent*, so every sample
  overstates by one request round-trip (a few ms locally).
- The membership path is observed at authz-svc's enforcer only. orgs-svc's
  own routes deny instantly via its local membership check, so they can't
  show propagation.
- Keycloak is replaced by a fake IdP. Token validation is the same code path,
  but JWKS fetch latency against a real Keycloak isn't measured.
